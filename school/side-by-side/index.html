01

How the calculations are counted

One bounce (or sweep) of one colour on this room is 26 × 26 = 676 multiply-adds: every patch gathers from every patch. Three colours, so a full bounce is 2,028. Counters in the code add 676 each time a 26 × 26 product runs. Nothing is estimated.

The guided side pays for its guarantee: checking the certificate is one more product (676) per colour per sweep, and it is in its total. Building the scene and the exact answer (a direct solve, 26³ = 17,576 per colour) are the same for both and counted for neither; the exact answer is only there to measure error.

02

Traditional is not made stupid

100 bounces is VRAD's default, and for these rooms it is genuinely enough. The left side's error is about 10⁻¹⁵, far inside the target. Nothing on the left is wrong. It just can't know when it's done, so it always spends the whole budget, and from darkness, because nothing carries over.

—

03

What the right side knows

After every sweep it computes the residual r = e + Ax − x and the bound ‖x* − x‖ ≤ ‖r‖/(1 − c), where c is the largest row sum of the light matrix. When that bound is under τ it stops. The guarantee needs only c < 1; ρ is shown for scale.

On an edit it starts from the light already on the walls, so a small change costs a few sweeps. When lamps move it gets at most a few sweeps per tick and chases. The bound says how close it is, every tick.
