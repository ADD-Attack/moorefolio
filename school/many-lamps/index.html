01

What you are looking at

The room from the first page: 8 metres by 5, its boundary cut into 26 one-metre patches. Now you can add bulbs, spotlights and lasers in any colour, at any brightness, anywhere inside, and the walls have colours too. Each lamp throws its light onto the patches once. That is bounce 1. After that the room takes over, and every bounce each patch passes on what reached it.

Colour runs as three separate rooms that share one shape: a red one, a green one and a blue one. A red wall reflects plenty of red and little else. Light that leaves it lands pinkish on the white walls next to it. That is colour bleeding, and the Colour bleed scene shows it arriving bounce by bounce.

The verdict under the room runs the theorem once per colour. In Channels split the walls reflect more red than they receive, so red runs away while green and blue settle. The theorem is saying three independent things about three independent rooms.

02

How the model is built

The theorem governs the room; lamps live in the emission. Einstein's theorem (DISCOVERY-1 S4, Wall.sharp_radiosity) is about the matrix A: the iteration xk+1 = e + A xk settles for every emission e exactly when ρ(A) < 1, exactly when a certificate exists. Lamps only change e. Moving, recolouring or dimming a lamp never changes whether a colour settles. Only the surfaces do that.

Colour. Each channel c has its own matrix Ac = diag(ρc)F, where a surface's ρc is its reflectivity times its colour in that channel. F is the exact crossed-strings form-factor matrix from the first page. Each channel runs its own iteration, and each gets its own ρ, certificate and verdicts.

Lamps. A lamp's light lands on the patches according to a distribution d, the share falling on each patch, with d ≥ 0 and Σd = 1. A lamp adds brightness × colourc × d to ec. From any point inside, the 26 patches split the full circle of directions between them. A bulb sends equal light in every direction, so each patch gets its angle's share of the full turn, and every patch gets some. A spotlight does the same across a 40° cone (20° each side of its aim), and only patches inside the cone get light. A laser puts all of it on the one patch its beam hits. The ceiling panel is the first page's lamp: a glowing patch whose own light is bounce 1.

What the numbers do. The Original room is the first page's default reproduced bit for bit: ρ = 0.6939 in all three colours, and by bounce 30 the shrink factor reads 0.6939 too. Because every step is linear, two lamps together give exactly the sum of each lamp alone (here to within 2 × 10⁻¹⁶). In Channels split every surface reflects 1.10 of red, 0.88 of green and 0.605 of blue, so ρ is 1.10, 0.88 and 0.605.

How the picture maps brightness. The numbers are exact; the picture is a display of them. Each bounce, the brightest patch in any colour, not counting patches that are themselves lamps, becomes the screen's white point W. A patch with value x in a colour is drawn at log(1 + 8x/W) ÷ log 9 of full brightness in that colour. So no wall patch is ever clipped, and a patch with more light always looks brighter. Only a ceiling panel, which is a lamp, can go past white. The glow inside the room is drawn on the same curve from the light arriving at each point, scaled to its own brightest point and capped at 40% of full brightness so the walls, which carry the numbers, stay the brightest things in the picture. A runaway room never washes out, because W grows with it. Hover or tap a wall to read its true values.

Honest limits: lamps are pure emitters. They don't block or reflect light. Direction matters only on the first flight; after that, light is tracked per patch, not per ray. The cone and beam drawn on the room are pictures of that first flight. Reflectivity above 1 isn't physical; it is here so you can cross the boundary.

03

Things to try

Superposition. Put a red laser and a blue spotlight on the same wall. Where they overlap, the patch reads the red one plus the blue one, exactly. Light adds.

Brightness can't break it. In the Original room, turn the lamp up to 3. Everything gets three times brighter, and the shrink factor and every verdict stay where they were, because those belong to the room.

A colour that runs away. Paint one wall pure red and push its reflectivity past 1. Watch the red verdict turn while green and blue stay settled. Or start from Channels split and pull all four reflectivities down to 0.90: red settles too.
