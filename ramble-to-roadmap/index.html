The field gate

Everything starts with what field you're in.

The first question we ask — before any analysis — is the one everything else keys off: what field is your business in? Not just the industry. The sub-field. "Multi-location dental group" produces a different roadmap than "dental," and a guessed field produces a wrong one.

So our strategy stage doesn't run on a guess. It waits — fail-closed — until you've confirmed the field in your own words. It's the cheapest question in the engagement, and it unlocks the expensive work. That's the deal: you ramble first, unconstrained. Then we confirm the field. Then the roadmap gets built on solid ground.

Buyer questions

Common questions, answered straight.

What does the audit cost, exactly?

$2,500, fixed. It credits 100% toward a build or Operate engagement started within 60 days of delivery — so if you move forward, you've already paid for the first step. If you don't, you walk away with a prioritized implementation plan specific enough to act on independently.

What if the audit finds nothing worth automating?

Then the fee is refunded. If your finished roadmap contains zero opportunities that clear the value bar defined in the report's ranking criteria, we don't keep money for a report that tells you to do nothing. We don't manufacture findings to justify a fee — that's the whole point of the nothing-invented rule.

Do I own the roadmap?

100%. Build it with us, build it with someone else, or build it in-house. The roadmap is yours regardless of what you decide next.

Why no questionnaire and no workshops?

Because questionnaires get the polished version, and workshops get the meeting version. We want the real version — how the operation actually runs, in your words. The structured work is our job, not your homework. The only thing we schedule live is the Day-5 decision call.

Why do you ask about my "field" first?

Because everything keys off it. The bottlenecks worth flagging, the risks that matter, the tools in your world, what "good" looks like — all field-specific. Confirming industry + sub-field is one short question, and it is the difference between a roadmap built for your kind of business and generic advice with the nouns swapped. Our strategy stage literally does not start until it's confirmed.

What happens with my information?

Your intake is used to build your report — only that. It is not sold, and it is not used to train anything for other clients. The report includes its own audit trail: every recommendation points back to what you said or a named source.

How much of my time does this take?

One intake (as long as your ramble is), one short round of numbered questions in an email thread, and one decision call on Day 5. That's the design: your involvement concentrates where judgment matters.

What happens after I receive the roadmap?

You decide. If you want to credit the audit toward a build or Operate, we scope it together from the roadmap — the roadmap already contains the requirements. If not, the plan is yours and there's no follow-up pressure. The 60-day credit window is the only clock, and it's stated up front.

How is this different from hiring a consultant, or a dev shop?

Consultants advise but don't build. Dev shops build but start from a spec you have to write. This starts from your own description of your operation and ends with a buildable, costed sequence — produced by the same pipeline that will help build it.

Is this going to recommend chatbots?

No. It's about identifying where automation creates real business value in your operation and which opportunities deserve investment first. If the answer is a $50/month tool you already could buy, the report tells you that.
