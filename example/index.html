Specialty

AI tooling, minus the hype

John builds and runs his own AI agent setups every day — so the advice is not theoretical. The specialty is practical AI for small teams: assistants that answer to a person, workflows that do defined work, and automation with a human holding the leash.

  • Assistant setups — a personal or team AI assistant, configured for your work, your files, your rules.
  • Agent workflows — multi-step jobs broken into agent-sized pieces, with checkpoints where a person decides.
  • Automation — the repetitive digital chores (reports, follow-ups, data wrangling) handed to software that shows its work.
  • Plain-English handover — what it does, what it costs to run, and where its limits are, in writing.

Process

Talk, demo, build, ship

A process sized for a one-person shop and a busy client: few meetings, early proof, no surprises at invoice time.

Talk

You explain the problem in plain language. John asks the questions a builder asks — what's it for, who uses it, what breaks if it stops — and says honestly whether he's the right tool for the job.

Demo

Before the real build, you get something you can see and click: a demo site, a prototype, a working slice. Decisions get made looking at the thing, not imagining it.

Build

The build proceeds in visible steps, with the same person writing the code who heard the problem. Scope changes get priced in the open, before they happen.

Ship & Support

It ships, it gets documented in plain English, and the person who built it answers when you call. Fixes and follow-ons go to the front of a very short line.
